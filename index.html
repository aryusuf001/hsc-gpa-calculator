The 4th Subject GPA Contribution Rule Explained

According to the Board of Intermediate and Secondary Education rules, your 4th subject works to boost your score safely without penalizing your average pool if you score weakly. Specifically, 2 points are deducted from your earned optional grade value. The points that remain over 2.00 add straight to your cumulative total before dividing your points pool across your 6 mandatory subject rows.

Example: If you hit an A+ (5.00) in your chosen optional class, 3 points (5.00 - 2.00) pool straight into your core metrics. If you achieve a C (2.00) or under, it contributes zero additional points to your tally, but does not drag your average down.

Critical Failure Warning

Please note that if you hit an F grade (0.00) in any mandatory main subject row, your aggregated global performance drops strictly to a GPA 0.00, indicating an overall fail result. This calculator correctly accounts for this dependency to prevent inaccurate estimates.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

What happens if I fail in my 4th subject in HSC?

If you fail (get an F grade) in your 4th or optional subject, you will still pass the overall HSC examination provided you pass all your mandatory subjects. An F grade in your optional track contributes 0 points to your average pool, but it never drags down your mandatory core results.

Can I get a total GPA of 5.00 if I get an A- in a main subject?

Yes! If your 4th subject scores an A+ (5.00), it yields a surplus of 3.00 points (5.00 - 2.00). These 3.00 bonus points can cover grade point shortages across your main subjects, successfully bumping your overall average up to a GPA 5.00.

How are the marks split for ICT in HSC 2026?

Information and Communication Technology (ICT) is evaluated out of a maximum of 100 marks total (combining Creative, MCQ, and Practical segments). Core group subjects span across two separate 100-mark paper sets totaling 200 marks maximum.
